# Brand Naming Research — Preliminary Collision Screen

**Status:** Active research; no final brand selected  
**Date:** 2026-08-18  
**Issue:** #3

## 1. Decision so far

Neither **Grovia** nor **AisleFlow** is suitable as a public product/company name.

- `Grovia` already has multiple public business/software uses and earlier research found software-related naming/trademark conflicts.
- `AisleFlow` is especially unsuitable: current public products use AisleFlow for wedding software/CRM and a grocery-management application already uses the exact name.

Therefore both remain internal historical research labels only.

This file is a **preliminary public collision screen, not trademark legal clearance**.

## 2. Naming strategy

The final brand should not describe only organic food or groceries. We want one initial grocery product, but the business may later support adjacent ecommerce verticals.

The name should be:
- distinctive rather than descriptive;
- pronounceable internationally;
- reasonably short;
- visually ownable;
- not confused with WooCommerce/WordPress themselves;
- able to sit above future products/extensions;
- searchable without competing against a dictionary/generic commerce phrase;
- screened for software/ecommerce businesses, WordPress products, domains and trademarks before launch.

## 3. Strategic naming territories

### Territory A — Flow without friction

Idea: continuous shopping, low interruption, fast store operation.

Avoid obvious names such as `StoreFlow`, `ShopFlow`, `CartFlow`, `FlowCart`, which are generic/high-collision spaces.

### Territory B — Craft / quality of store-building

Idea: a product built carefully for store owners rather than a pile of theme options.

Words such as `Craft`, `Wright`, `Foundry`, `Forge` communicate quality but are crowded. Exact combinations require strong collision checks.

### Territory C — Rhythm / repeat commerce

Idea: everyday purchasing, repeat shopping, customer habits, continuity.

This connects to our Household Rhythm UX thesis but should not make the company sound like a consumer grocery app only.

### Territory D — Structure / navigation

Idea: making a complex store easier to move through and easier to configure.

`Aisle` is useful conceptually but is too grocery-specific for the parent brand and already creates collision risk.

### Territory E — Neutral coined parent brand

Idea: a distinctive invented word with no literal grocery meaning, allowing product names underneath it later.

This currently appears to be the strongest territory because commerce compounds and `-flow/-shop/-store/-market` combinations are highly occupied.

## 5. Research candidates — NOT cleared

### Kaveliq

**Status:** preliminary candidate only.

Current broad public searches did not surface an obvious active software/ecommerce product using the exact word. Search noise did surface unrelated URL-token text, which is not a meaningful brand collision.

Pros:
- coined/neutral;
- can support a parent company and products below it;
- not limited to grocery;
- visually distinctive spelling.

Concerns:
- pronunciation is not instantly obvious (`ka-VEH-lik` vs alternatives);
- terminal `q` can feel like generic startup naming if the identity is not strong;
- absence from broad web search is **not trademark/domain clearance**.

Decision: keep only as a research candidate; do not brand the product around it yet.

## 6. Names/territories deliberately deprioritized

Even where an exact collision did not immediately appear, we should avoid overusing:
- `Aisle*` — too grocery-specific and AisleFlow already collides;
- `Basket*` — strongly grocery/consumer-app oriented, limiting parent-brand expansion;
- `Shop*`, `Store*`, `Market*`, `Cart*` — very crowded and difficult to own/search;
- generic `-ora`, `-io`, `-iq` constructions without a defensible story;
- names that look like generated ThemeForest product names.

## 7. Next naming sprint

Do not generate hundreds of random names.

For the next pass:
1. choose 2–3 brand-character territories (precise / calm / capable / fast, etc.);
2. create 20–30 names with explicit pronunciation and story;
3. remove names that sound too close to established commerce/software brands;
4. run exact web, WordPress.org, ThemeForest/Woo, GitHub, company and domain screens;
5. run official trademark database screens for the strongest 3–5 finalists in priority classes/jurisdictions;
6. check linguistic meaning/pronunciation in India + major international markets;
7. only then choose a legal-clearance shortlist.

## 8. Naming gate remains closed

No name in this document is legally cleared.

Do not:
- buy an expensive domain;
- commission a final logo;
- rename customer-facing packages;
- publish a marketplace identity;
- file a trademark;

until the shortlist passes deeper collision/trademark review.

## 9. Product lesson

The naming research reinforces the same principle as our UX research: **do not choose the first thing that sounds polished.** The final brand needs evidence of distinctiveness just as the product needs evidence of customer value.
