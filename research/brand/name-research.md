# Brand Naming Research — Collision Screen

**Status:** Active research; no final brand selected  
**Updated:** 2026-08-19  
**Issue:** #3

## 1. Decision so far

Neither **Grovia** nor **AisleFlow** is suitable as a public product/company name.

- `Grovia` already has multiple public business/software uses and earlier research found software-related naming/trademark conflicts.
- `AisleFlow` is especially unsuitable: current public products use AisleFlow for wedding software/CRM and a grocery-management application already uses the exact name.

Therefore both remain internal historical research labels only.

This file is a **public collision screen, not trademark legal clearance**.

## 2. Naming architecture

- **BhaivaTech** remains the publisher/company identity.
- The WooCommerce product should receive its own distinctive product brand.
- The product brand does not need to literally contain `grocery`, `organic`, `shop`, `store`, `market`, `cart`, `woo`, or `WordPress`.
- Public copy can explain the category: `<Product> — grocery-first WooCommerce theme by BhaivaTech`.

This lets the product be memorable without asking the name itself to explain every feature.

## 3. Naming strategy

The final product brand should be:
- distinctive rather than descriptive;
- pronounceable internationally;
- reasonably short;
- visually ownable;
- not confused with WooCommerce/WordPress themselves;
- searchable without competing against a dictionary/generic commerce phrase;
- strong enough for future extensions below the same product family;
- screened for software/ecommerce businesses, WordPress products, domains and trademarks before launch.

## 4. Strategic naming territories

### Territory A — Flow without friction

Idea: continuous shopping, low interruption, fast store operation.

Avoid obvious names such as `StoreFlow`, `ShopFlow`, `CartFlow`, `FlowCart`, which are generic/high-collision spaces.

### Territory B — Craft / quality of store-building

Idea: a product built carefully for store owners rather than a pile of theme options.

Words such as `Craft`, `Wright`, `Foundry`, `Forge` communicate quality but are crowded. Exact combinations require strong collision checks.

### Territory C — Rhythm / repeat commerce

Idea: everyday purchasing, repeat shopping, customer habits, continuity.

This connects to the repeat-commerce thesis without making the brand sound like a consumer grocery app only.

### Territory D — Structure / navigation

Idea: making a complex store easier to move through and easier to configure.

`Aisle` remains useful conceptually but is too grocery-specific for the main brand and already creates collision risk.

### Territory E — Neutral coined product brand

Idea: a distinctive invented word with no literal grocery meaning, with the category explained by the descriptor/tagline.

This remains the strongest territory because commerce compounds and `-flow/-shop/-store/-market` constructions are highly occupied.

## 7. Surviving research candidates — NOT cleared

A candidate surviving broad indexed-web screening means only that an obvious collision was not found. It does **not** mean the domain is available or that the name is legally registrable.

### Kaveliq

**Working pronunciation:** `ka-VEH-lik`  
**Status:** strongest current research candidate, not a recommendation to launch.

Pros:
- coined and neutral;
- short enough for product UI/package naming;
- not tied to grocery-only terminology;
- broad indexed searches still have not surfaced an obvious active exact-name software/ecommerce brand.

Concerns:
- pronunciation is not self-evident;
- terminal `q` risks looking like generic startup naming;
- official trademark/domain/linguistic clearance remains undone.

### Cadeliq

**Working pronunciation:** `ka-DELL-ik`  
**Status:** second-pass research candidate only.

Story:
- loosely derived from **cadence**: everyday commerce should feel continuous and repeatable rather than interruptive.

Pros:
- connects subtly to repeat-commerce/product rhythm without using grocery clichés;
- broad indexed searches did not surface an obvious active exact-name software/ecommerce product during this pass;
- visually distinct from current grocery-theme competitors.

Concerns:
- pronunciation may vary (`ka-DELL-ik`, `KAY-deh-lik`);
- again uses a terminal `q`, so Kaveliq and Cadeliq should not both survive merely because the construction searches cleanly;
- official trademark/domain/linguistic clearance remains undone.

## 8. Why there are only two survivors

The target was not “produce five names at all costs.” Most names that sounded polished failed quickly because modern software/ecommerce naming space is extremely crowded.

Keeping only two survivors is preferable to padding the list with weaker names. The next sprint should produce a **non-`q` naming territory** so the final legal-clearance set has structural diversity rather than five variants of the same startup spelling pattern.

## 9. Next naming gate

Before either survivor can become a finalist:

1. create a second coined territory that avoids terminal `q` entirely;
2. retain only 1–3 additional names that pass broad collision screening;
3. run exact/fuzzy checks in WordPress.org, Envato/ThemeForest, GitHub and relevant app/software directories;
4. check `.com` and practical alternate domains through authoritative registrar/RDAP data;
5. run official trademark-database searches in priority jurisdictions/classes, including India and likely international software/commercial classes;
6. check pronunciation/unintended meaning in India + major English-speaking markets;
7. take the strongest 3–5 to professional trademark counsel before material brand spend.

## 10. Naming gate remains closed

No name in this document is legally cleared.

Do not:
- buy an expensive domain;
- commission a final logo;
- rename customer-facing packages;
- publish a marketplace identity;
- file a trademark;

until the shortlist passes deeper collision/trademark review.

## 11. Product lesson

The naming research reinforces the same principle as the UX research: **do not choose the first thing that sounds polished.** Distinctiveness needs evidence just as customer value needs evidence.
