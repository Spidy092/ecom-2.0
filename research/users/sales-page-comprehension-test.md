# Sales Page V0 — 30-Second Comprehension Test

**Status:** Ready for target-buyer sessions; no participant results yet  
**Date:** 2026-08-18  
**Issue:** #16

## Goal

Test whether the sales page communicates the product and its value before visual branding or checkout is built.

This is not a preference test. Do not ask whether the page is "nice" until after comprehension questions.

## Participants

Prioritize people who plausibly choose or recommend WooCommerce themes:
- WordPress/WooCommerce freelancers;
- small agency builders;
- ecommerce implementers;
- store owners who have previously selected a WordPress theme.

Aim for at least 5–8 initial participants across the above groups before locking the core message.

## Message variants

Use the same page with one query parameter:

### Variant A — shopping speed

`index.html?variant=A`

> Build the grocery store people can shop quickly.

### Variant B — smaller stack / launch

`index.html?variant=B`

> Launch a serious grocery store without assembling a plugin pile.

### Variant C — repeat grocery behavior

`index.html?variant=C`

> Make next week’s grocery order easier than this week’s.

Rotate variants across participants rather than showing all three before the first comprehension test.

## First exposure

1. Start at the top of the page.
2. Give the participant **30 seconds** to look naturally.
3. Do not explain the product.
4. Hide/close the page after 30 seconds.
5. Ask the unaided recall questions below.

Do not force scrolling. Where the participant chooses to look is part of the evidence.

## Unaided recall questions

Ask in this order:
1. What do you think this product is?
2. Who is it for?
3. What seemed different from other WooCommerce themes/products?
4. What software/plugins did you understand it to require?
5. What did you think was included?
6. Did you notice a price? If yes, what was it and was it one-time or recurring?
7. What made the product seem trustworthy or untrustworthy?
8. What would stop you from trying or buying it?

Do not correct wrong answers until all unaided questions are finished.

## Guided follow-up

Reopen the page and ask the participant to:
- find the interactive shopping-flow proof;
- find required dependencies;
- find what is included;
- find the pricing hypothesis;
- explain what happens after subscription expiry;
- explain what the product does **not** provide.

Record where they look first and where terminology causes confusion.

## Scoring

### Product identity

Pass if participant identifies something substantially equivalent to:

> paid/premium WooCommerce theme/product for grocery/organic/local-food stores.

### Differentiation

Pass if participant independently recalls at least one core difference:
- faster multi-item grocery basket flow;
- repeat-shopping / Buy Again behavior;
- early delivery/serviceability certainty;
- smaller required plugin stack / no mandatory Elementor;
- grocery-specific rather than generic multipurpose design.

### Required stack

Pass if participant understands:
- WordPress;
- WooCommerce;
- our theme;
- our companion core plugin;
- Elementor is **not mandatory**.

### Scope boundaries

Fail if participant leaves believing the product includes:
- hosting;
- payment processing;
- delivery fleet/driver dispatch;
- custom client development.

### Commercial model

When price has been viewed, pass if participant understands:
- working hypothesis: $59/year;
- one production store;
- updates/support are entitlement benefits;
- existing installed product is not remotely disabled after expiry.

## Initial internal thresholds

Do not treat these as public claims:
- >=80% identify grocery + WooCommerce target;
- >=70% recall a real differentiator;
- >=80% understand the product is Theme + Core rather than hosting/SaaS;
- 0 participants believe Elementor is mandatory after reviewing requirements;
- 0 participants believe we provide payment processing or a delivery fleet after reviewing boundaries.

If fewer than five qualified participants have been tested, treat percentages as directional only.

## Variant decision

Do not choose the headline solely by "favorite" votes.

Rank variants by:
1. correct product identity;
2. correct target-customer recall;
3. differentiator recall;
4. absence of misleading assumptions;
5. interest/credibility after comprehension;
6. fit with actual V1 product evidence.

A memorable headline that causes wrong product assumptions loses.
