# V1 Research Recruitment Messages

These messages are intentionally neutral. Do not promise that the product is faster, better, easier, or “revolutionary” before the participant tests it.

## Grocery shopper — short message

> Hi! I’m testing an early mobile grocery-shopping prototype and need a few people who already buy groceries online. It’s not a finished product and there’s nothing to buy. I’d like you to try a realistic shopping task while I observe where the prototype is clear or confusing. I’m testing the product, not you. If you’re interested, I’ll send the test link and instructions.

## Grocery shopper — WhatsApp-friendly

> I’m doing research on a new grocery-shopping experience. I need a few people who normally order groceries on their phone to try an unfinished prototype. No purchase/payment is involved. I mainly want to see where the experience is easy or confusing. Interested?

## WooCommerce freelancer/developer

> Hi! I’m researching a new grocery-first WooCommerce product before we write the production theme/plugin. I’m looking for a few people who have built or maintained WooCommerce stores to critique the product direction, setup model, dependency strategy, and what would make a new paid theme credible enough to use. This is research, not a sales pitch. Would you be willing to give practical feedback?

## Store owner/operator

> Hi! I’m researching an early ecommerce product for grocery/food stores. I’m looking for store owners/operators who have dealt with choosing, setting up, or maintaining an online store. I’d like to understand what causes the most setup/support pain and show you an unfinished shopping prototype. There’s nothing to purchase; I’m trying to learn what would actually be valuable before building the commercial product.

## What NOT to say before testing

Avoid:

- “This is faster than WoodMart/Bacola.”
- “We solved grocery UX.”
- “This will be the best WooCommerce theme.”
- “Our Buy Again feature is amazing.”
- “We only need 17 clicks.”
- “Would you pay $59 for this?” as the opening question.

Those statements bias the session and turn research into confirmation-seeking.

## Screening questions — shopper

Ask briefly before scheduling:

1. Do you buy groceries online at least once a month?
2. Do you usually shop from a phone, desktop, or both?
3. Which grocery apps/sites do you use most often?

Do not reject someone for preferring a competitor; that perspective is valuable.

## Screening questions — builder

1. Have you built or maintained a WooCommerce store?
2. Which commercial/free themes or builders have you used recently?
3. Have you been responsible for updates, plugin conflicts, performance, or support issues?

## Screening questions — store owner/operator

1. Have you been involved in choosing or operating an ecommerce website?
2. Who normally handles website changes/updates for you?
3. What is the biggest recurring problem with the current store/site setup?

## Research incentive

If we later offer a gift/incentive, it should compensate for the participant’s time, not depend on giving positive feedback or publishing a testimonial.
