## 1. Current evidence boundary

We now have:
- competitor/product research;
- automated accessibility smoke checks;
- deterministic fixed-mission interaction counts;
- mobile structural reconnaissance;
- an interactive low-fidelity prototype;
- a local-only anonymous session exporter;
- six human pilot exports;
- two mobile-sized shopper traces at 400 × 874;
- direct qualitative feedback from at least one shopper about the primary flow and terminology confusion.

We still **do not have enough evidence** for production because:
- the fixed shopper missions were not followed consistently;
- individual observation notes are incomplete;
- three exports use `anonymous` rather than a unique participant code;
- buyer willingness-to-pay evidence is incomplete;
- store-owner/setup expectations are not synthesized;
- manual screen-reader/real-device accessibility findings remain incomplete;
- no evidence yet proves Aisle Rail is better than conventional navigation.

Therefore the production gate remains closed.

## 4. Human findings so far

### Primary grocery loop

**Evidence:** participants exercised delivery, search, aisle navigation, add, quantity changes, cart-related surfaces, saved/list behavior, and repeat-product behavior. No pilot export demonstrates a blocking failure of the basic add/quantity/cart loop.

**Current interpretation:** promising, but not yet validated for task efficiency or preference.

### Repeat shopping mental model

At least one shopper could not explain what the returning-shopping experience meant.

The previous labels included:
- `Returning`;
- `Household rhythm`;
- `This week`.

**Decision:** revise shopper-facing terminology to:

```text
Buy again
Products you bought before
```

`Returning` may remain an internal research mode only.

### Saved items versus current purchase

At least one shopper could not clearly distinguish `List` from `Cart` / `Basket`, or explain the save/remove behavior.

One mobile returning trace also contains several rapid save toggles on the same product. Event data alone cannot tell us why, but paired with the direct feedback it increases the priority of testing clearer terminology.

**Decision:** use only two shopper-facing container concepts:

```text
Saved
Saved for later
Save for later
Remove from saved
```

and

```text
Cart
Your current cart
View cart
Cart updated
```

Avoid mixing `List`, `Basket`, and `Cart` during the next round.

## 6. Core hypotheses

### H1 — Delivery certainty belongs before cart investment

**Current status:** exercised, value still unproven.

Evidence for:
- delivery checks appear in multiple traces;
- mobile first-time trace used delivery before the first add.

Evidence against:
- no structured observation yet shows that early serviceability materially reduces confusion/trust risk.

Decision: **KEEP FOR NEXT TEST; NEEDS EVIDENCE.**

### H2 — Aisle Rail improves grocery navigation

**Current status:** used, superiority unproven.

Evidence for:
- aisle changes appear in real traces.

Evidence against:
- no controlled comparison with conventional categories/navigation.

Decision: **KEEP FOR NEXT TEST; NEEDS EVIDENCE.**

### H3 — Product Ledger makes multi-item shopping faster without confusion

**Current status:** operable; human efficiency unproven.

Evidence for:
- participants added and changed quantities without a product-detail page requirement.

Evidence against:
- pilot missions were inconsistent, so interaction/time values cannot demonstrate faster shopping.

Decision: **KEEP; TEST WITH FIXED MISSION.**

### H4 — Persistent cart feedback improves continuous shopping understanding

**Current status:** cart surfaces were exercised; terminology needed correction.

Evidence for:
- participants used cart/pulse navigation.

Evidence against:
- `Basket` versus `Cart` wording added avoidable ambiguity.

Decision: **REVISE LANGUAGE TO CART; RETEST.**

### H5 — Repeat shopping materially improves weekly grocery shopping

**Current status:** concept not yet understood clearly enough.

Evidence for:
- returning product controls were exercised.

Evidence against:
- at least one shopper did not understand the previous `This week / Household rhythm` concept.

Decision: **REVISE TO BUY AGAIN; RETEST BEFORE PRODUCTION.**

### H6 — Block-first / no mandatory Elementor is commercially attractive

**Current status:** architecture accepted; buyer desirability still unproven.

Decision: **NEEDS BUILDER/BUYER EVIDENCE.**

### H7 — Small required-plugin surface is purchase-relevant

**Current status:** strategic hypothesis, not yet validated through buying behavior.

Decision: **NEEDS BUILDER/BUYER EVIDENCE.**

## 9. Round-1 decision

- [ ] **PROCEED** — interaction thesis is understood/valuable enough for production engineering.
- [x] **REVISE** — prototype revision required before engineering.
- [ ] **REJECT / CHANGE THESIS** — current concept lacks meaningful advantage.

### Rationale

The pilot did not reveal a blocking failure in the primary shopping loop, so there is no evidence-based reason to abandon the concept. It did reveal a meaningful language/mental-model problem around repeat shopping and saved items, and the research procedure was inconsistent enough that efficiency numbers cannot yet support a production decision.

Therefore we revise terminology, run a disciplined mobile round, then reassess.

## 10. Next-round protocol

Before the next participant:
- use the revised prototype labels: `Buy again`, `Saved for later`, and `Cart`;
- use a unique code (`S05`, `S06`, etc.);
- use a mobile device or approximately 390 × 844 viewport;
- follow the fixed mission exactly;
- create a short observation note in addition to the JSON export;
- ask what `Buy again`, `Saved`, and `Cart` mean **before explaining them**.

Target for next decision:
- at least **5 relevant mobile shopper sessions** on the revised prototype;
- ideally a mix of first-time and returning missions;
- separate builder/store-owner evidence for the commercial/implementation hypotheses.

## 11. Production gate

Production WordPress/WooCommerce implementation remains locked until:
- the revised shopper terminology has been tested with mobile shoppers;
- fixed-mission observations are synthesized;
- critical manual accessibility issues are addressed or explicitly scoped;
- builder/store-owner evidence is sufficient for the block-first/small-stack commercial assumptions;
- the Round-1 decision is changed from `REVISE` to `PROCEED` with evidence;
- PRD/architecture are updated if the evidence materially changes product scope.
