# Grovia Lean Business & Launch Plan

**Principle:** one strong product -> first paying customers -> revenue/feedback -> expand.

## 1. Business definition

Grovia is planned as a premium WooCommerce product business, beginning with one grocery-first commercial theme + core plugin.

We are not building a large software company before validating demand. V1 should be operable by a very small team using ChatGPT/Codex for acceleration and GitHub automation for repeatable engineering work.

## 2. Initial positioning

Working positioning:

> A fast, modern WooCommerce theme built specifically for everyday grocery shopping.

Working promise:

> Launch quickly. Shop quickly. Maintain easily.

The launch message should emphasize grocery workflow, mobile UX, setup simplicity, performance, and low dependency burden — not raw demo/widget count.

## 3. First target market

Primary categories:
- grocery;
- organic food;
- farm/fresh produce;
- local food retailers.

Primary buyers:
- store owners;
- WordPress freelancers;
- small agencies.

## 4. Competitive benchmark set

Track at minimum:
- Bacola;
- GreenMart;
- Freshio;
- Organio;
- newer grocery-specific products;
- WoodMart as a long-term quality/market benchmark rather than the initial direct positioning target.

Maintain a quarterly or pre-major-release competitor snapshot covering price, update cadence, sales/review signals, builders/dependencies, mobile UX, grocery-specific features, support/documentation, and visible security/maintenance signals.

## 5. Product-market validation ladder

### Milestone A — paid validation

1 unrelated customer pays.

### Milestone B — early validation

10 paying customers with documented onboarding/support outcomes.

### Milestone C — evidence

50 paying customers; identify repeated objections, support issues, and conversion blockers.

### Milestone D — before broad expansion

100 paying customers and evidence that the core product deserves additional starter sites/features/plans.

Do not use vanity metrics such as social likes as a substitute for payment/retention/support evidence.

## 6. Pricing hypothesis

Initial hypothesis:
- US$59/year;
- 1 production website;
- updates while subscription is active;
- standard support while subscription is active.

Do not launch multiple complicated plans until customer behavior justifies them.

Potential later plans (not V1 commitments): 5-site and agency tiers.

## 7. Distribution priority

### Tier 1

1. Own product website/demo/docs/checkout.
2. Direct content/SEO/tutorials.
3. Agency/freelancer outreach and early beta relationships.

### Tier 2

4. WordPress.org acquisition path if/when a Lite product is strategically justified.
5. Relevant commercial marketplaces when onboarding/submission is available and economics make sense.

The business must not depend on one marketplace.

## 8. Current marketplace constraint

At planning time (2026-08-18), Envato's public guidance says new author applications are temporarily paused. Therefore ThemeForest is a future optional channel, not a V1 launch dependency.

Re-verify this status before launch; do not hard-code the assumption into long-term planning.

## 9. Sales site minimum

Before taking payment, we need:
- clear product landing page;
- live Modern Grocery demo;
- features/benefits page;
- pricing/license explanation;
- documentation/getting-started;
- compatibility requirements;
- changelog;
- support/refund policy;
- security/reporting page;
- checkout/account/update delivery solution;
- privacy/terms and required business disclosures.

## 10. Marketing before launch

Do not wait until engineering is finished.

During build:
- publish design/performance/build decisions selectively;
- collect an early-access list;
- recruit a small number of real WooCommerce builders/store owners;
- create educational content around grocery WooCommerce UX and performance;
- record setup and shopping-flow demos as they become stable.

## 11. Launch content themes

High-intent topics to research/validate:
- grocery WooCommerce theme;
- organic food WooCommerce theme;
- fast WooCommerce grocery store;
- mobile WooCommerce grocery UX;
- how to build an online grocery store with WooCommerce;
- WooCommerce grocery delivery postcode/zone setup;
- WooCommerce repeat-order/buy-again workflows.

Avoid publishing thin SEO pages only to chase keywords.

## 12. Support as product research

For every support conversation categorize:
- installation/setup;
- compatibility;
- bug;
- performance;
- UX confusion;
- missing feature;
- customization request;
- billing/license;
- documentation gap.

Repeated support questions should first trigger a product/docs improvement, not immediately a new support hire.

## 13. Automation-first operating model

### ChatGPT

Use for:
- product reasoning and competitive synthesis;
- PRD/requirements refinement;
- UX critique;
- documentation planning;
- marketing/support knowledge synthesis;
- release communication drafts.

### Codex

Use for:
- small issue-scoped implementations;
- tests;
- refactoring;
- code investigation;
- documentation updates tied to code;
- PR preparation/review assistance.

### GitHub Actions

Use for:
- lint/static checks;
- tests;
- security/dependency checks;
- deterministic package builds;
- release artifact generation;
- later deployment of docs/demo where appropriate.

Human review remains required for product, security-sensitive, licensing, pricing, and release decisions.

## 14. Hiring rule

Do not hire to solve a process that can first be simplified or automated. Hire when a validated revenue/customer bottleneck requires sustained human judgment or throughput.

Likely first human leverage areas after traction:
- exceptional UI/visual designer;
- senior WordPress/WooCommerce engineering review;
- customer support/QA.

This is not a commitment to hire before revenue.

## 15. Expansion gate

New starter sites, builders, plugins, SaaS services, marketplace features, or agency portals require evidence from one or more of:
- repeated paying-customer request;
- measurable conversion/retention impact;
- significant support-cost reduction;
- defensible strategic distribution opportunity.

No expansion based only on "competitor has it."

## 16. Legal/business checklist before paid launch

Obtain professional advice where required for jurisdiction-specific business/tax matters. Operational checklist:
- entity/invoicing/tax setup;
- business bank/payment account;
- terms;
- privacy policy;
- refund policy;
- support policy;
- product licensing compatibility;
- third-party license inventory;
- demo image/font/icon redistribution rights;
- trademark/domain review for final brand;
- security disclosure process;
- data processing/vendor list.

## 17. The only V1 business objective

Build a product good enough that the first 10 real customers choose to pay for it and can successfully use it. Everything after that should become easier to prioritize because we have evidence instead of guesses.
