# Brand & Naming Research

**Status:** Early research — do not treat as legal clearance  
**Date:** 2026-08-18

## 1. Working codename

`Grovia` is currently only an internal project codename.

Do not purchase branding assets, publish a marketplace identity, register domains, or build customer recognition around this name until formal naming clearance is complete.

## 2. Why the codename is risky

Preliminary public research found multiple existing Grovia/GroVia businesses, including:
- an established GroVia consumer-products brand;
- an India-based AI/web/automation company using Grovia;
- an AI/business software product using Grovia;
- other consulting/technology businesses using Grovia;
- a public GROVIA software-related trademark record.

This does not by itself determine legal infringement in every jurisdiction/class. It is enough to fail our product's **uniqueness** standard and justify a new final name.

## 3. Naming requirements

The final name should be:
- distinctive rather than descriptive;
- easy to pronounce internationally;
- reasonably short;
- not tied so narrowly to grocery that future adjacent commerce products become awkward;
- compatible with WordPress/theme/plugin marketplace naming conventions;
- visually ownable as a brand;
- available or realistically obtainable across core domains/social/repository identities;
- screened for confusingly similar software/ecommerce/WordPress marks.

## 4. Naming anti-patterns

Avoid endless variants of:
- organic + suffix;
- green + suffix;
- grocery + suffix;
- shop + suffix;
- woo + suffix;
- farm + suffix;
- names confusingly close to Organio/Organico/Organia/Organiqo or other existing marketplace products.

These names make differentiation harder and increase confusion/search/trademark risk.

## 5. Naming process

### Stage A — strategic territories

Create 4–6 naming territories derived from our actual advantage, for example:
- fast repeat shopping;
- fresh/local commerce;
- effortless store operation;
- dependable everyday purchasing;
- flow/movement/basket behavior;
- a neutral parent brand that can expand beyond grocery.

### Stage B — candidate generation

Generate a broad set of original candidates without checking domain availability first; this avoids letting mediocre available domains drive the brand.

### Stage C — collision screen

For finalists check:
- exact web search;
- fuzzy/similar spelling search;
- WordPress.org;
- Woo/ThemeForest marketplaces;
- GitHub;
- major app stores/software directories where relevant;
- social handles;
- domain options;
- company/business-name collisions;
- trademark databases in priority markets/classes.

### Stage D — linguistic screen

Check pronunciation, unintended meanings, transliteration issues, and confusing spelling in primary target markets.

### Stage E — professional legal clearance

Before material brand investment, use qualified trademark/legal advice for priority jurisdictions and goods/services classes.

## 6. Current decision

Until Stage E is complete:

> **Grovia = internal codename only.**

Repository paths may temporarily use the codename; customer-facing identity must wait for the final naming decision.
